**The Deity of Christ** is not just a historical treatise—it is a living challenge to modern Adventism and Christianity. Longacre’s work calls readers to examine the foundation of their faith: Who is Christ? What does it mean that He is the only-begotten Son of God? In an era when the identity of Christ and the nature of God are often blurred or redefined, this book returns to the original, pioneer understanding—rooted in Scripture and the lived experience of the 1844 movement.

Longacre warns that doctrinal drift and organizational compromise threaten the very core of the Adventist message. He documents how the pioneers’ clear, Bible-based view of Christ was gradually replaced by creedal statements and institutional control. His message is urgent: the authority of Scripture, the reality of Christ’s risk and sacrifice, and the call to “stand on the old paths” are not optional—they are essential for spiritual survival in the last days.

This book matters today because it exposes the roots of apostasy, calls for a return to authentic faith, and reminds us that the truths discovered in the Great Disappointment are still present truth. It is a summons to courage, clarity, and a living connection with the Son of God.

         <div style="font-size:1.13rem;color:#d9deee;margin-bottom:1.2rem;">
            Charles Smull Longacre (1871–1958) was a Seventh-day Adventist minister, author, and administrator. Born near Valley Forge, Pennsylvania, he became active in church leadership and religious liberty work. Longacre was a key figure in the denomination during the early 20th century and served as secretary of the Religious Liberty Association for decades.
            <br><br>
            In 1899, he married Lulu Belle McClure in Elkhart, Indiana. He taught at South Lancaster Academy and edited Liberty magazine. Longacre is best known for his book, <em>The Deity of Christ</em>, which addressed the nature of Christ and the Godhead.
            <br><br>
            Importantly, Longacre worked for the April 15, 1904 “new organization” (see <a href="/april-15-1904-new-organization" style="color:#fcd34d;font-weight:600;">1SM 204</a>), a turning point in Adventist church structure. His career and writings reflect the tensions and changes of this era. For more on the denominational shift, see <a href="/apostasy" style="color:#fcd34d;font-weight:600;">Apostasy: Resume of Betrayal</a> and <a href="/april-15-1904-new-organization" style="color:#fcd34d;font-weight:600;">Image of the Papacy</a>.

### 🔨 The Final Strike

Longacre’s voice still echoes:

- The pioneers found the truth
- He defended the truth
- The later system departed from the truth

And now comes the dividing line:

Will you return to the foundation laid in tears, or remain in a structure warned would fall?
